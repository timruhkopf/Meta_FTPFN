# References

Papers worth remembering *in relation to this project*, per the convention in `.claude/skills/references/SKILL.md` — not a general bibliography; every entry below connects to a specific mechanism, baseline, or open question in `docs/ROADMAP.md` / `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## Privileged information / auxiliary supervision

### Learning Using Privileged Information: Similarity Control and Knowledge Transfer ([link](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume16/vapnik15b/vapnik15b.pdf))

**Idea:** Vapnik & Izmailov's LUPI framework: at training time, in addition to the standard input-output pairs (x, y), the learner also gets access to *privileged* information x* about each example (e.g. a teacher's explanation, extra sensor data, or the correct answer's derivation) that will not be available at test time. They formalize how a "student" (e.g. an SVM) can use a "teacher" trained on x* to build a knowledge-transfer term — SVM+ — that reweights or corrects the student's loss, giving faster convergence than learning from (x, y) alone.

**Relevance / limitations:** Our generative prior hands the model true privileged information at training time that will never be available for a real transfer pair at inference — the ground-truth warps S_A, S_B and the exact pushforward targets A_inB(x), B_inA(x) (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §2, §6.2). The auxiliary registration/coupling losses (§6.2–6.3, and their deep-supervised counterparts in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §3.2–3.3) are structurally a LUPI-style construction: extra supervision that shapes an intermediate representation but is absent at deployment. The limitation is real, though: LUPI's classical machinery (SVM+, similarity control via a slack-rescaling teacher) is built for a fixed teacher/student pair on the *same* input distribution, with the privileged channel used to reweight per-example importance — it has no notion of a coordinate warp between the privileged source and the target, and no amortized/in-context inference story. Our setting needs the latter two, so LUPI is the right lens for *why* the auxiliary losses should help, not a source of any specific mechanism to reuse.

### Learning by Cheating ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294)) — [repo](https://github.com/dianchen96/LearningByCheating)

**Idea:** Chen, Zhou, Koltun & Krähenbühl train autonomous-driving policies in two stages. A "privileged agent" first learns to drive from ground-truth state (exact positions of the road, lanes, and other agents) — an easy problem since the confounds of visual perception are removed. That privileged agent then acts as a teacher, supervising a second, purely vision-based student agent via imitation, so the student never has to *jointly* solve perception and control.

**Relevance / limitations:** This is a concrete instance of the general LUPI pattern applied via a two-stage teacher/student split rather than a joint loss, and it maps well onto our §3.4 "oracle distillation": we already plan a second, teacher-forced forward pass (transport fixed to ground truth) whose output distribution the real model is distilled against via KL, which is exactly "train a privileged agent, then have it teach the student" instantiated as one model with shared weights instead of two networks. The disanalogy: Learning by Cheating's privileged agent solves a genuinely *easier* problem (control given exact state) than the student's problem (control from pixels) — the two agents are asymmetric in inputs but not in what they're being asked to predict. Our oracle pass predicts the exact same p(y|x*) as the student; only the transport is privileged, so the connection is closer to LUPI's original framing than to this paper's two-network teacher/student split, and no imitation-learning-specific machinery (e.g. DAgger-style distribution correction) is applicable here.

## Domain adaptation & cycle-consistency

### CyCADA: Cycle-Consistent Adversarial Domain Adaptation ([link](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/hoffman18a/hoffman18a.pdf)) — [repo](https://github.com/jhoffman/cycada_release)

**Idea:** Hoffman et al. (ICML 2018) adapt a model from a source domain to a target domain by combining pixel-level adaptation (a CycleGAN-style generator that stylizes source images to look like the target, and vice versa) with feature-level adaptation, and tie the two together with a cycle-consistency loss: translating source → target → source (or the reverse) should recover the original image. The combination lets the method enforce both perceptual realism and semantic-content preservation without paired source/target examples.

**Relevance / limitations:** The relevant idea here is architectural, not the GAN machinery: CyCADA needs *both directions* of the source ↔ target map to be learned and mutually consistent, which is the same shape as our role-randomization training (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §2's B_inA head trained alongside A_inB "at no extra generative cost", and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4.3's swap-encoder/decoder training) and the deferred cycle-consistency diagnostic in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §8 (`T̂_{B→A}(T̂_{A→B}(x_i)) ≈ x_i` as a test-time check with no ground truth needed). The limitation: CyCADA is unpaired, adversarial, and pixel/feature-space — it has no generative model of the warp itself and no identifiability argument (no analogue of our §1.3 gauge-fixing assumption `f_A = f_B ∘ T`), so cycle-consistency there is the *entire* training signal for the mapping, whereas for us it would only ever be a diagnostic/auxiliary term layered on top of supervised registration targets that our latent-z prior already provides for free. Not a baseline to reconstruct — a precedent for why cycle-consistency is a meaningful and previously load-bearing signal for bidirectional domain maps, worth stealing as a diagnostic.

## Meta-learning & transfer for Bayesian optimization

### Robust Transfer for Bayesian Optimization with Prior-Data Fitted Networks ([link](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Ge3wbgb2Vi)) — Li, Daulton, Müller, Wilson & Bakshy; accepted at the 3rd SPIGM Workshop @ NeurIPS 2025 (PDF at `external/92_Robust_Transfer_for_Bayesia.pdf`)

**Idea:** Multi-Task Prior-Data Fitted Networks (MTPFNs): a PFN trained on a *multi-task* data-generating process that mixes related and unrelated auxiliary tasks with a per-task "is this task related at all" probability `p`, so the trained network learns to discount irrelevant auxiliary data instead of being told which tasks matter. Architecturally, a hierarchical-attention transformer with a `[Task]` summary token per source: intra-task blocks attend within each task's own points, inter-task blocks attend across the per-task summary tokens (not the raw points), reducing attention cost from O(D²T²) to O(D²+T²) where D is points-per-task and T is the number of tasks. Evaluated as a BO surrogate on HPOBench and tabular FC-Net benchmarks, showing robustness to negative transfer from unrelated tasks and matching fully-Bayesian (NUTS) multi-task GP inference at a fraction of the cost (~0.5s vs 352s/iteration).

**Relevance / limitations:** This is a **baseline we need to reconstruct** — it is the closest published system to "amortize multi-task/related-transfer inference inside a PFN for BO surrogate modeling" and sits right next to what this project is attempting; see `docs/ROADMAP.md` §8.4 (baseline ladder) and the "Open items" list, both updated to point back here. Key structural differences worth tracking during reconstruction: (1) MTPFN's tasks all share the **same input coordinate system** — there is no warp/diffeomorphism between task A and task B, and no registration problem to solve. Its hierarchical attention answers "how much should I trust this other task's *data*", never "where does this other task's *coordinate frame* sit relative to mine", which is the entire subject of our project (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §1.3 gauge fixing, §5 architecture). (2) MTPFN's relevance-gating is scalar and per-task (a single `p` per auxiliary task); it has no equivalent of our per-point transport/coupling heads, fold statistic, or registration bottleneck (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §5.4, §2.3c). (3) It is a workshop paper (SPIGM @ NeurIPS 2025), not yet a fully-reviewed venue publication, and the released comparisons are against GP-based multi-task baselines (ICM, ScaML), not against any registration-aware method — so it doesn't itself answer whether task misalignment (our actual problem) breaks it, which is a gap our Experiment 0 (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §8.1) could directly probe by feeding MTPFN's own DGP through our warp family.

### A Quantile-based Approach for Hyperparameter Transfer Learning ([link](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/salinas20a/salinas20a.pdf)) — Salinas, Shen & Perrone; ICML 2020 (PMLR 119)

**Idea:** Cross-task transfer for BO/HPO via a semi-parametric Gaussian Copula, aimed squarely at the *y*-side of the problem: different tasks' objective functions have heterogeneous, often non-Gaussian scales and noise levels, which breaks naive standardization. For each task, replace every observed `y_i` with its empirical-CDF rank `F(y_i)` (Winsorized near 0/1 to avoid `Φ⁻¹` blowing up at the extremes), then push through the inverse Gaussian CDF: `z = Φ⁻¹(F(y))`. By the probability integral transform this maps *every* task's `y`-distribution onto the same `N(0,1)`, regardless of its original shape — the "copula" is exactly this shared-Gaussian-margin construction. A single joint MLP `θ` is then fit across all source tasks' pooled `(x, z)` pairs, predicting `N(μ_θ(x), σ_θ(x)²)`, and used either directly for Thompson sampling (Copula Thompson Sampling) or as an informative prior mean/covariance for a GP that also adapts online to the new task (Gaussian Copula Process). Extends to multi-objective BO by scalarizing multiple copula-transformed objectives (e.g. latency + accuracy).

**Relevance / limitations:** This is a specific, well-tested answer to the "unknown monotone `h`" half of our own `f_A = f_B ∘ T` split (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`/`docs/ROADMAP.md` §1.5's y-distortion, and the still-unbuilt P3 rung, §8.6) — theirs is a fully nonparametric per-task rank transform to a shared Gaussian, ours (in the still-planned P3 extension, and in `warpbench/docs/experiments/hpo-warp-complexity.md`'s real-data measurement study) constrains `h` to a low-parameter family (`a·YeoJohnson(y;λ)+b`). Worth reading against `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-11-hpo-warp-h-family-constrained-to-yeojohnson.md` directly: that entry retracted an unconstrained isotonic `h` specifically because it could absorb a real *x*-warp `T`'s effect, making severity look artificially small. Salinas et al.'s fully nonparametric empirical-CDF `h` sidesteps that failure mode only because their setting has **no `T` at all** — every task shares the exact same config space with no coordinate warp between tasks, so there is nothing for an unconstrained `h` to confound with. It is not evidence that a nonparametric `h` is safe once a genuine `x`-distortion is also in play; if anything it's a clean illustration of why our own constraint was necessary. Also relevant to `docs/ROADMAP.md` §8.4's "1-D quantile matching" baseline row (a closed-form axis-aligned registration baseline) — this paper is the same quantile-matching idea applied to `y` instead of `x`, and could be read as a template for a `y`-only analogue of that baseline once P3 exists to evaluate it against. Also the "Rung L1" reference point in `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` §1/§3: `T = id`, `h` free is the standard transfer-HPO assumption, and this paper is that assumption done properly — the direct baseline any richer rung (L1.5 and up) has to beat.

### Transfer Learning based Search Space Design for Hyperparameter Tuning ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02511)) — Li, Shen et al.; 2022 (arXiv:2206.02511)

**Idea:** Defines cross-task similarity as the fraction of order-preserving configuration pairs between a source task's surrogate and the target task's own observations (a rank-agreement statistic), arguing that in HPO the partial ordering over configurations — and which region is promising — matters more than agreement in raw performance values. Uses the resulting similarity to size a promising search-space region for the target task (a box/region search-space-design method, not just a similarity score).

**Relevance / limitations:** States the rationale for `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md`'s Rung L1 ("rank consistency is the null model") explicitly and independently of Salinas et al. — a second, converging source for "ranking, not raw values, is the right invariant at this rung." Its use of the similarity score to size a *region* (rather than fit a monotone `h` and stop) is a step toward Rung L2 (region families, `J(q)`) but stays a single box, not a nested family of top-`q` regions at varying `q` — the ladder's L2 rung (and Watanabe et al. below) is the more direct precedent for that.

### Rank/surrogate-weighted transfer HPO family — Bardenet et al. (2013); Yogatama & Mann (2014); Golovin et al. (2017, Google Vizier); Feurer, Letham & Bakshy (2018, RGPE)

**Idea (as a family, not one paper):** Bardenet et al. use a ranking-based surrogate to make cross-task collaborative HPO robust to scale differences without full standardization. Yogatama & Mann fit an offset-corrected GP across tasks (a per-task additive correction rather than a rank transform). Golovin et al. (Vizier) stack GPs trained on earlier studies' residuals as priors for a new study. Feurer, Letham & Bakshy's RGPE (Ranking-Weighted Gaussian Process Ensemble) weights an ensemble of per-source-task GP surrogates by how well each source's *ranking* of a held-out sample of the target's own observations agrees with the target's true ranking — i.e. Rung L1's rank-agreement statistic, used as a literal per-source mixture weight rather than a global relatedness report.

**Relevance / limitations:** Of the four, RGPE is the closest existing thing to *operationalizing* a relatedness measure rather than only reporting one — it turns Rung L1 into a per-source scalar that directly controls how much a source task's surrogate contributes to the target's posterior. None of the four have any notion of a coordinate warp between tasks (Yogatama & Mann's offset correction is the closest to a `T`, and it is additive/global, not a genuine per-point diffeomorphism) — all four assume the same shared config space with, at most, a scale/rank mismatch on `y`. **Citation confidence:** flagged by the requester as recalled from memory rather than independently re-checked in this pass — venues/years above are as given and worth confirming before treating any specific numeric claim from these four as settled.

### Gaussian Copula Process ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2010.68)) — Wilson & Ghahramani; IEEE TPAMI, 2010

**Idea:** Introduces the Gaussian Copula Process: instead of assuming a GP's marginals are Gaussian, model an arbitrary (possibly non-Gaussian) marginal per input via a learned monotone warping function into a latent Gaussian space, where an ordinary GP applies. Handles heteroskedastic, non-Gaussian, single-task data without hand-picking a likelihood.

**Relevance / limitations:** Cited by Salinas et al. above as a way to alleviate scale/non-Gaussianity issues *within a single task*, at the extra cost of estimating the marginal CDF — Salinas et al.'s own contribution is extending exactly this device *across* tasks. Directly relevant to where the acquisition/optimization bias in a copula-based method actually enters: the CDF estimation step, not the copula itself. Single-task by construction — no notion of transfer or of a second, related data source at all.

### Speeding Up Multi-Objective Hyperparameter Optimization by Task Similarity-Based Meta-Learning for TPE ([IJCAI 2023](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0602.pdf)) — Watanabe et al.

**Idea:** Builds a task kernel for meta-learned TPE from a "γ-set similarity" measure: the intersection-over-union of two tasks' top-`γ` (best-performing) configuration sets. Uses this task-kernel similarity to weight how much a meta-learned TPE model borrows from each prior task when modeling a new one.

**Relevance / limitations:** This *is* `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md`'s Rung L2 statistic (`J(q)`, IoU of top-`q` regions), already published and already used operationally as a task kernel — `γ` plays the role of the ladder's `q`. The paper reports degraded performance in high-dimensional spaces and in some transfer settings, consistent with the ladder's own observation (§1) that the top-`q` region stops being a good local descriptor once dimensionality grows. Two things the ladder does that this paper doesn't: report the whole curve `J(q)` over a range of `q` rather than one fixed `γ`, and compute the region from a denoised surrogate mean over a uniform grid rather than from raw observations (noisy at small `γ`/`q`).

### Learning Search Spaces for Bayesian Optimization ([arXiv:1904.09880](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09880)) — Perrone, Shen, Seeger, Archambeau & Jenatton; 2019

**Idea:** Learns a restricted search space (a low-volume hyper-rectangle or hyper-ellipsoid) that contains the optimal configurations of a set of related, previously-tuned tasks, then runs ordinary BO restricted to that learned region for a new task — trading the full box for a small region trusted to contain good configurations.

**Relevance / limitations:** Cited by Salinas et al. above as an alternative, non-meta-feature route to defining task similarity/transfer. Relative to `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md`'s Rung L2, this is a cruder, single-level, axis-aligned version of the region-family comparison: one fixed region (a box or ellipsoid) rather than a nested family of top-`q` regions at varying `q`, so it can't show *where* (at what `q`) two tasks' good regions start to diverge the way the ladder's `J(q)` curve can. **Citation confidence:** recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass.

### Input Warping for Bayesian Optimization of Non-Stationary Functions ([arXiv:1402.0929](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0929)) — Snoek, Swersky, Zemel & Adams; ICML 2014

**Idea:** Warps the input space of a single BO task through a per-axis Beta-CDF transform before feeding it to a GP, motivated by the observation that objective functions are typically far more sensitive to hyperparameters near the good region than far from it — a non-stationarity that a stationary-kernel GP handles poorly, and that a known-in-advance log-transform only fixes when the non-stationarity direction is known ahead of time.

**Relevance / limitations:** This is `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md`'s Rung L2.5 mechanism (axis-wise monotone input warps) applied *within one task* to handle its own non-stationarity, not *between* tasks to align two tasks' coordinate systems — the ladder's L2.5 rung is the cross-task analogue of this idea, which does not appear to exist in the transfer-HPO literature (see the ladder log's own note on this gap). Reusable machinery, wrong axis of application for our purposes as published. **Citation confidence:** recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass.

### Observation-dependent Bayesian active learning via input-warped Gaussian processes ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10644)) — Jarl, Bånkestad, Scragg & Sjölund

**Idea:** Injects observation-dependent feedback into active learning by warping the GP's input space with a learned monotone reparameterization fit to observed data, letting the sampling/design policy expand sparsely-sampled or highly variable regions and compress well-understood ones, rather than using a fixed acquisition geometry.

**Relevance / limitations:** Single-task, like Snoek et al. above, but the mechanism — a monotone `x`-warp fit to data that then changes downstream (acquisition) behavior — is directly reusable machinery for a cross-task L2.5-style warp. Doesn't address cross-task transfer or relatedness measurement at all; the contribution we'd borrow is the fitting/parameterization approach for a monotone warp, not the active-learning framing it's embedded in.

### AutoML Loss Landscapes / algorithm-configuration landscape unimodality — Pushak & Hoos; PPSN 2018 (algorithm configuration, best paper) and *AutoML Loss Landscapes*, ACM TELO 2022 (ML extension)

**Idea:** Tests one-dimensional parameter-response slices (vary one parameter, hold the rest fixed at a good configuration) for unimodality/convexity, across solver-runtime landscapes (2018: SAT/TSP/MIP algorithm configuration) and later ML hyperparameter landscapes (2022). Finds nearly every slice studied is statistically indistinguishable from unimodal at 95% significance, with a small number of exceptions, but also that it is *not* safe to assume parameters don't interact — occasional strong, compensatory interactions are hypothesized to explain the exceptions.

**Relevance / limitations — corrected after checking, see `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-citation-check-pushak-vanrijn-mutransfer.md` for the full reasoning.** Originally cited here (and in `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` §5) for "HPO landscapes tend toward unimodality and are benign" — that over-states what's shown. "Statistically indistinguishable from unimodal" under a noisy landscape is failure-to-reject, not confirmation; the evidence is slice-based (axis-wise unimodality does not imply joint unimodality — a function unimodal along every coordinate slice through a point can still have off-axis basins); and the 2018 paper's domain is algorithm configuration, not ML hyperparameters (the 2022 TELO paper is the actually-relevant extension). The compensatory-interaction finding is itself evidence *against* naive axis-wise benignness — it's exactly the curved-valley case that makes basin *shape* (not just presence of one basin) the object worth measuring. Checked against every framework component that touches unimodality (`warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-citation-check-pushak-vanrijn-mutransfer.md`'s dependency table): most of the ladder (the quadratic local analysis, `J(q)`/Rung L2, the `T`-free pre-checks) don't actually need a global unimodality assumption; EVT tail-exponent fitting and practical registration-optimization convergence are the two places it's genuinely load-bearing.

### Hyperparameter Importance Across Datasets ([arXiv:1710.04725](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04725)) — van Rijn & Hutter; KDD 2018

**Idea:** Functional ANOVA across ~100 OpenML datasets (SVM, random forest, AdaBoost) shows which hyperparameters matter and which value ranges perform well *generalizes* across datasets — e.g. for AdaBoost, max tree depth (then learning rate) explains most of the variance and is significantly more important than the rest by a Nemenyi test, consistently across tasks.

**Relevance / limitations — corrected after checking, see `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-citation-check-pushak-vanrijn-mutransfer.md`.** Originally cited here as a source for "importance shifts across tasks", framed as a geometric obstruction to a diffeomorphism existing between two tasks. That's backwards: stable importance across tasks is *good news* for a warp-based approach — it means the active subspace is roughly shared, which is exactly the condition the dimensional reduction in `docs/labbook/2026-09-14-dimensionality-sample-efficiency-dof.md` §5 needs to be valid. The actual geometric obstruction (importance *crossing zero* between tasks — a hyperparameter mattering on A but not on B changes whether the good region touches a box face, which no diffeomorphism of the box can reconcile with a task where it doesn't) is a standalone argument that doesn't need this paper to state; van Rijn & Hutter's result instead suggests that specific failure mode is *rarer* than the original framing implied. Worth checking directly via per-task fANOVA on our own benchmarks for hyperparameters whose importance crosses zero across tasks.

### μTransfer / Tensor Programs V — Yang, Hu et al.; "Tensor Programs V: Tuning Large Neural Networks via Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer", NeurIPS 2021 ([arXiv:2203.03466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03466))

**Idea:** Under the Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) — width-dependent scaling of per-layer learning rates and initialization — the optimal learning rate (and other hyperparameters) becomes approximately width-independent, in contrast to standard parameterization where it drifts systematically with width. Lets hyperparameters be tuned once on a small proxy network and transferred zero-shot to a much larger one.

**Relevance / limitations — see `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-citation-check-pushak-vanrijn-mutransfer.md` for the fuller argument.** This is not primarily a transfer-HPO method to reuse but a demonstration that **relatedness, as this project defines it, is coordinate-dependent**: the same pair of tasks (width-256 vs. width-4096 networks) can measure as "moderately related, shifted optimum" (`T ≠ id`, real energy) under standard parameterization and "essentially identical" (`T ≈ id`, energy ≈ 0) under μP — same tasks, different declared coordinates, different relatedness number. Formally, reparameterizing by `R` sends `T ↦ R T R⁻¹`, and a diffeomorphism's registration energy is not invariant under that conjugation. Consequences: (1) energies are only comparable within one fixed, declared coordinate space — never across differently-scaled boxes — which is *why* declared bounds matter for reproducibility, not because they make the measure canonical; (2) a low energy can mean "related" or "well-chosen coordinates," indistinguishable without a sensitivity check; (3) **an idea flagged but not yet built**: minimizing energy over a reparameterization group, `R*(A,B) = min_{R∈𝒢} E(R T_{A→B} R⁻¹)`, turns "these tasks look unrelated" into "we haven't found the right coordinates yet" and gives a coordinate-free relatedness measure — restricted to axis-wise monotone `𝒢` this is exactly `warpbench/docs/labbook/...measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md`'s Rung L2.5, reframed as a canonical form (the same move as the Francos `𝓡` operator below, applied to a structured reparameterization subgroup instead of the radiometric group).

### Learning Hyperparameter Predictors by Dataset Meta-Features Distance ([Wistuba, Schilling & Schmidt-Thieme, 2015](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7364046)) and DTW-on-PCA-meta-feature initialization (Neural Computing & Applications, 2022)

**Idea (as a pair):** Wistuba et al. define task similarity purely from dataset meta-features (number of classes/samples/features, etc.) — no reference to the response surface at all — to select which prior tasks' surrogates to transfer from. The 2022 dynamic-time-warping paper does the analogous thing over PCA-reduced meta-feature *curves* (a sequence, warped in the DTW sense over meta-feature-space, not config-space).

**Relevance / limitations:** Both sit entirely outside `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md`'s ladder — relatedness there is defined from meta-features, never from `x`/`y` response-surface agreement at all. Useful as a contrast baseline: if a surface-based relatedness measure (the ladder's own approach) doesn't predict realized transfer gain any better than a meta-feature distance does (`warpbench/docs/labbook/...measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` §8's "does relatedness predict transfer gain" check), that's a real, informative negative result about how much the extra registration machinery buys. **Citation confidence:** both recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass — DTW paper's exact title/venue in particular is unconfirmed.

### Landscape structure behind the ladder's dimensionality argument — Bergstra & Bengio (2012); Bergstra & Bengio, "Random Search for Hyper-Parameter Optimization" is the usual cite for low effective dimensionality of hyperparameter spaces

**Idea:** Empirically, hyperparameter optimization landscapes tend to have low *effective* dimensionality — a small number of hyperparameters explain most of the variation in performance, even when the nominal search space is high-dimensional.

**Relevance / limitations:** Load-bearing for `docs/labbook/2026-09-14-dimensionality-sample-efficiency-dof.md` §5's active-subspace reduction (project the warp onto the top eigenvectors of `E[∇g_B ∇g_B^T]`, which is exactly where Fisher information doesn't vanish) — this paper is the empirical justification for expecting that subspace to actually be low-dimensional in practice, not just as a convenient assumption. Distinct from, and not to be conflated with, the *unimodality* claim above (Pushak & Hoos) — low effective dimensionality and joint unimodality are separate properties, and only the former is this paper's actual claim. **Citation confidence:** recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass.

### Perrone, Jenatton, Seeger & Archambeau (2018), "Scalable Hyperparameter Transfer Learning" (ABLR) and Swersky, Snoek & Adams (2013), "Multi-Task Bayesian Optimization"

**Idea (as a pair):** ABLR shares a neural-network feature extractor across tasks with a per-task Bayesian linear-regression head on top (a shared representation, not a shared coordinate warp). Multi-Task BO instead models correlated tasks jointly with a multi-task GP (e.g. an intrinsic coregionalization model), sharing statistical strength across tasks' surrogates without any explicit `x`-space alignment.

**Relevance / limitations:** Both are baselines this project's own formulation generalizes in the sense of "share information across tasks with a shared coordinate frame" — ABLR shares a learned feature space, MTBO shares a joint kernel — but neither has any notion of *warping* one task's input coordinates into another's; both assume the tasks already live in one shared space. Useful comparison points for whether an explicit registration/warp mechanism buys anything over a purely shared-representation or shared-kernel approach. **Citation confidence:** both recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass.

## Geometric/radiometric image registration

The classical vision literature for jointly decoupling a geometric (coordinate) deformation from a radiometric (intensity/value) transformation between two signals — directly relevant to this project's own `x`-distortion (`T`) / `y`-distortion (`h`) split, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`/`docs/ROADMAP.md` §1.5, and `docs/labbook/2026-09-14-problem-setting-h-and-T-identifiability.md`'s identifiability analysis. This literature has the machinery and the identifiability results; the HPO transfer-learning literature above has the motivation and the benchmarks; as of this pass, nothing found connects the two.

### Decoupled Linear Estimation of Affine Geometric Deformations and Non-Linear Intensity Transformations of Images ([IEEE TPAMI 32(5), 2010](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4967941)) — Kovalsky, Cohen, Hagege & Francos (also a 2007 SSP workshop paper for the noisy case, and US patent 8,275,204)

**Idea:** For `h(x) = Q(g(A(x)))` with `Q` an increasing invertible (radiometric) map and `A` affine (geometric), shows the joint high-dimensional, non-convex problem of recovering both deformations simultaneously reduces to an *exact* sequence of two linear systems — solvable regardless of the deformations' magnitude, no local minima. The key device is the **sample distribution transformation** (the "quantile gauge" in our own terms), `[𝒯g](t) = λ{x ∈ supp g : g(x) ≤ t} / λ{supp g}` — essentially "normalize by your own value's empirical CDF." Their Lemma 1 shows this transform is *invariant* to right-composition by an affine map and *covariant* to left-composition by an increasing map, giving `𝒯h = [𝒯g] ∘ Q⁻¹` (solve radiometry first) and, with `ℛh := [𝒯h] ∘ h − [𝒯h](0)`, `ℛh = [ℛg] ∘ A` (solve geometry first) — a formal statement of "quantile-normalize both signals, and what's left over is exactly the geometric map." Also recovers `|A|` (the affine map's determinant/scale) in closed form, and the result is robust enough to initialize a subsequent nonlinear optimizer, reducing the chance of that optimizer converging to the wrong local minimum.

**Relevance / limitations:** This is the central reference for the project's own quantile-gauge construction (`warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` §4: `h := F_A⁻¹ ∘ F_B` from surrogate-on-grid CDFs) — for genuinely affine `T`, this paper shows that gauge is the *exact* decoupling, not a heuristic; for a general (non-affine) `T`, our own use of the same gauge is a convention whose error is governed by the non-constancy of `|det DT|`, which is why the ladder log reports `log|det DT|` statistics as an explicit error budget rather than assuming exactness. The authors' own stated limitation — generalizing to elastic (non-affine) geometric deformation and non-invertible intensity mappings is left as future work — is precisely the gap this project's own diffeomorphic (velocity-field) `T` and general monotone `h` sit in; nobody has published the elastic/diffeomorphic version of this exact-decoupling result.

### Parametric Estimation of Affine Transformations: An Exact Linear Solution ([Journal of Mathematical Imaging and Vision, 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10851-009-0180-0)) — Hagege & Francos

**Idea:** The geometry-only linear solver used inside the Kovalsky/Cohen/Hagege/Francos construction above — recovers an affine geometric transformation between two signals exactly, in closed form, without needing to jointly resolve a radiometric transformation.

**Relevance / limitations:** Supporting/component reference for the entry above rather than independently applicable — the affine-only special case. Confirmed via the citation trail inside the main Kovalsky et al. paper, not independently fetched and read in this pass.

### Hagege & Francos (2005), SSP workshop — optimal moment-functional selection under model mismatch

**Idea:** Addresses how to choose the moment functionals `w` used by the linear affine estimator above so that the estimate degrades gracefully when the assumed model (exact affine geometry, exact monotone radiometry) doesn't perfectly hold — i.e. robustness of the linear solution under model mismatch and noise.

**Relevance / limitations:** Directly relevant to any application of this machinery to noisy HPO surrogate data, where the affine/monotone assumptions will only ever hold approximately. Confirmed via the citation trail inside the main Kovalsky et al. paper, not independently fetched and read in this pass.

### Francos, Hagege & Friedlander (2003), Asilomar Conference — the 1-D homeomorphism (radiometric) solver

**Idea:** The one-dimensional solver for recovering the increasing invertible map `Q` (the radiometric/intensity transformation) alone — the "homeomorphism version" of the joint estimator, i.e. the piece that handles the `y`-side transform in isolation.

**Relevance / limitations:** Component reference, the direct analogue of the still-unbuilt P3/`h`-estimation work in this project's own roadmap (§8.6) done via the same quantile-gauge machinery rather than a constrained parametric family. Confirmed via the citation trail inside the main Kovalsky et al. paper, not independently fetched and read in this pass.

### Geometry and Radiometry Invariant Matched Manifold Detection ([IEEE Trans. Image Processing 26(9), 2017](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7961152)) — Sharon, Hagege & Francos

**Idea:** Generalizes the affine/monotone decoupling to a detection/recognition setting: when an object undergoes both an affine geometric transformation and a monotonic radiometric transformation, and the family of possible deformations admits a finite-dimensional representation, there is a mapping from observation space into a low-dimensional linear space in which the entire orbit of "the same object under any allowed deformation" becomes a linear subspace.

**Relevance / limitations:** If relatedness were to be framed as a subspace distance (is B's response surface, under *some* allowed `T`/`h`, close to lying in the same low-dimensional orbit as A's) rather than as a fitted-warp energy, this is the relevant construction. Not currently how this project measures relatedness (`warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` uses fitted energy + nulls, not a subspace/orbit test), but a genuinely different formal route worth keeping in mind. Confirmed via the citation trail inside the main Kovalsky et al. paper, not independently fetched and read in this pass.

### Diffeomorphic Registration With Intensity Transformation and Missing Data ([Frontiers in Neuroscience / PMC7027169](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7027169/)) — Tward et al.

**Idea:** Penalized Bayesian estimation of a diffeomorphic geometric deformation *jointly* with an intensity/contrast transformation between a target image and a deformed atlas: contrast is modeled as a polynomial function of the atlas's intensity, and the deformation's velocity field is regularized by a Sobolev-type norm with operator `(id − a²Δ)⁴`. A first-order (linear) polynomial contrast model reduces exactly to normalized cross-correlation as the data term; higher-order polynomials allow non-monotone intensity swaps.

**Relevance / limitations:** The closest published system to this project's *full* model — a genuine diffeomorphism jointly estimated with a flexible `y`-transform, with an explicit regularizer on the deformation. The gauge (identifiability between the geometric and radiometric parts) is fixed *implicitly*, by restricting the contrast model to low-order polynomials — the direct analogue of this project's own choice to constrain `h` to a low-parameter family (Yeo-Johnson) rather than leave it fully nonparametric (`warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-11-hpo-warp-h-family-constrained-to-yeojohnson.md`). Unlike the Kovalsky/Francos line above, this paper does not analyze identifiability of the `Q`/`T` split formally — the restriction is a practical modeling choice, not a proven-exact decoupling.

### Deformable Medical Image Registration: A Survey ([IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging / PMC3745275](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3745275/)) — Sotiras, Davatzikos & Paragios

**Idea:** Survey of deformable image registration methods and their formal properties, including a clear statement of why registration is fundamentally ill-posed: at every position one is estimating a full displacement vector from only a scalar intensity constraint, so unknowns outnumber constraints almost everywhere. The canonical illustration: registering two discs — a translation aligning their centers, followed by *any* rotation, is an equally valid solution; nothing in the data disambiguates rotation.

**Relevance / limitations:** The standard-reference framing for exactly the kind of tangential/rotational degeneracy this project's own Fisher-conditioning argument (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §5.3/§8.5, and `docs/labbook/2026-09-14-problem-setting-h-and-T-identifiability.md`) is built to detect — a registration direction with no informative gradient behind it. Useful as the citable statement of the ill-posedness itself, not a method to reuse.

### Beg, Miller, Trouvé & Younes (2005), "Computing Large Deformation Metric Mappings via Geodesic Flows of Diffeomorphisms" — LDDMM

**Idea:** The foundational Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping construction: parameterize a deformation as the time-1 flow of a time-varying velocity field, and define a proper metric on the space of diffeomorphisms via the geodesic (minimal-energy) path between them.

**Relevance / limitations:** The velocity-field parameterization and metric that this project's own stationary-velocity-field `T` (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §4.1) is a simplified (stationary, not time-varying) special case of. **Citation confidence:** flagged by the requester as recalled from memory, confirmed only via appearing in Tward et al.'s own citations, not independently fetched and read in this pass.

### Devilliers, Allassonnière, Trouvé & Pennec — inconsistency of template estimation by register-and-average

**Idea (as recalled, unconfirmed):** Shows that estimating a population "template" image by iteratively registering individual samples to a running average and re-averaging is a statistically inconsistent estimator — registering noise systematically biases the estimated template toward being *too sharp* and biases estimated deformation variance.

**Relevance / limitations:** If this project ever estimates a "canonical" or "template" task response surface by registering many tasks to a running average (e.g. for a population-level relatedness study), this result would predict a specific, systematic bias. **Citation confidence: the least certain reference in this batch** — the requester flagged this as "the one I'd most want you to verify," confident in the result but not in the exact venue/year. Treat the exact citation as unconfirmed until checked directly; the result itself is a specific, checkable claim (register-and-average template estimation is inconsistent) that should be verified against the actual paper before being relied on.

### Roche, Guimond, Ayache & Meunier (2000), "Multimodal Elastic Matching of Brain Images", ECCV

**Idea:** Introduces the "monofunctional dependence" assumption for multimodal image registration — that one imaging modality's intensities are some (a priori unknown) function of the other modality's intensities at the same location — as the basis for a similarity measure robust to cross-modality intensity differences.

**Relevance / limitations:** The monofunctional-dependence assumption is exactly this project's own `h` under a different name, from an independent (multimodal medical imaging, not geometric/radiometric decoupling) branch of the registration literature. **Citation confidence:** confirmed only via appearing in the Francos line's own citations, not independently fetched and read in this pass.

### Comparametric equations (Mann, 1996; 2000) and Candocia (2003), "Jointly Registering Images in Domain and Range by Piecewise Linear Comparametric Analysis", IEEE Trans. Image Processing 12(4)

**Idea:** Mann's comparametric equations model the relationship between two differently-exposed photographs of the same scene as an unknown monotone function of intensity ("comparametric" function) to be estimated jointly with geometric alignment. Candocia's piecewise-linear approach approximates that function via a first-order (locally linear/Taylor) expansion for tractability.

**Relevance / limitations:** Directly the same `x`/`y` joint-estimation problem, from photography/HDR imaging rather than medical imaging or HPO. Worth reading specifically as a cautionary case: per Francos et al.'s own discussion, Candocia's first-order Taylor approximation is restricted by an implicit small-geometric-deformation assumption and provably fails for e.g. a 180-degree rotation — the same "tasks are already close" assumption any linearized approach to this project's own registration problem would be making (`docs/labbook/2026-09-14-problem-setting-h-and-T-identifiability.md` §2.3's own linearization). **Citation confidence:** confirmed only via appearing in the Francos line's own citations, not independently fetched and read in this pass.

## Neural processes / amortized conditional inference

### Conditional Neural Processes ([arXiv:1807.01613](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01613)) and Neural Processes ([arXiv:1807.01622](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01622)) — Garnelo et al.; 2018

**Idea:** Amortize conditional stochastic-process inference with a shared encoder mapping a variable-size context set to a fixed-size representation (CNP: deterministic; NP: a latent variable with an ELBO), and a decoder that queries that representation at new input points to produce a predictive distribution — a direct neural analogue of GP-style conditioning, trained once across many sampled functions rather than fit per function.

**Relevance / limitations:** The general lineage PFNs (`docs/REFERENCES.md`'s "Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference" entry) also belong to — amortized, in-context conditional prediction trained across a distribution of tasks/functions. NP's latent-variable ELBO route is the one PFNs deliberately avoid (a PFN's transformer context IS the conditioning mechanism, no explicit latent/ELBO). **Citation confidence:** recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass.

### Attentive Neural Processes ([arXiv:1901.05761](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05761)) — Kim et al.; 2019

**Idea:** Fixes CNP/NP's tendency to underfit the context set by replacing the mean-pooled context representation with cross-attention from each query point to the context points directly.

**Relevance / limitations:** The attention-based fix here is architecturally close to what a PFN already does natively (context tokens attended to directly via self/cross-attention, no pooling bottleneck) — useful mainly as evidence that mean-pooling a context set (as CNP/NP do, and as this project's own encoder bottleneck partially does, `docs/ROADMAP.md` §5.1) is a real capacity cost worth measuring, not a free simplification. **Citation confidence:** recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass.

### Autoregressive Conditional Neural Processes ([arXiv:2303.14468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14468)) — Bruinsma et al.

**Idea:** Produces valid joint predictive distributions over multiple query points by chain-rule factorization (autoregressively conditioning each new query on previously-predicted ones) rather than a shared latent variable — avoiding the ELBO gap of NP-style models, at the cost of the result depending on query order.

**Relevance / limitations:** A PFN's query tokens are mutually independent given the context by construction (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §3.1's mask), which sidesteps this paper's order-dependence problem entirely but also means a PFN never produces a genuine *joint* predictive distribution over multiple test points the way an autoregressive CNP does — worth keeping in mind as a capability this project's own model structurally forgoes. **Citation confidence:** recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass.

### JEPA (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture) — LeCun (position paper); Assran et al. (I-JEPA and successors)

**Idea:** Predicts a target's representation in a learned embedding space from a context, rather than predicting the target's raw values — the loss lives in representation space, not observation space, motivated as avoiding wasted capacity on unpredictable low-level detail.

**Relevance / limitations:** Raised specifically as a comparison point for this project's own test-test (query-query) attention design choice — worth checking directly against whichever design-doc section discusses that comparison before treating the connection as more than an architectural analogy; JEPA's actual training objective (representation-space prediction) is unrelated to PFN-style bar-distribution NLL over observation space. **Citation confidence:** recalled from memory, not independently re-checked in this pass, and the weakest-specified reference in this group (no single paper pinned down).

## Benchmarks (HPO / multi-fidelity)

### FCNet / Tabular Benchmarks for Hyperparameter Optimization — Klein & Hutter; 2019

**Idea:** Four UCI regression datasets (protein structure, slice localization, naval propulsion, Parkinson's telemonitoring), one shared 9-D discretized configuration space, exhaustively tabulated. Already used by the sibling `warpbench` repo's `docs/experiments/hpo-warp-complexity.md` measurement study (via its HPOBench wrapping) and by `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` §9 as the first benchmark to run the ladder on.

**Relevance / limitations:** Small `K=4` (12 directed pairs) — weak for any meta-feature regression, but the cleanest, smallest, fully-tabulated resource available, which is exactly why both the warp-complexity measurement study and the relatedness ladder pick it as the first correctness check before scaling up.

### LCBench / Auto-PyTorch — Zimmer, Lindauer & Hutter

**Idea:** A tabular multi-fidelity benchmark: ~35 OpenML datasets, a shared 7-D continuous+integer configuration space, learning curves over 1-52 epochs.

**Relevance / limitations:** Already vendored and used by `warpbench/docs/experiments/hpo-warp-complexity.md` (`LCBenchTabular`, confirmed there to share its random-search design exactly across tasks — see that doc's "Scaling up" implementation notes). `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` §9 flags datasets-*and*-epochs as letting the fidelity-chain consistency check run, but notes the shared-configuration claim should be reverified independently for that specific use.

### TaskSet — Metz et al.

**Idea:** A very large collection of optimization tasks (different models/losses) evaluated under a large, shared set of optimizer hyperparameter configurations — designed as a benchmark for meta-learning optimizers.

**Relevance / limitations:** Closest thing in this list to a dense meta-task distribution on a genuinely shared design, per `warpbench/docs/labbook/2026-09-14-measuring-task-relatedness-the-ladder.md` §9 — but `warpbench/docs/experiments/hpo-warp-complexity.md`'s own implementation notes found TaskSet's per-task loss scale heavy-tailed enough to break an MSE-based warp-fitting loss even after winsorization (rank-based fits survive fine); that specific finding should be taken into account before relying on TaskSet for anything beyond rank-based statistics (Rung L0/L1) in the relatedness ladder either.

## PFNs as surrogates / amortized inference

### Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10510))

**Idea:** Müller, Hollmann, Pineda Arango, Grabocka & Hutter introduce Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs): train a single transformer, once, to minimize NLL over many synthetic datasets sampled from a prior p(D) = ∫ p(D|h)p(h)dh; at inference, one forward pass over a new (context, query) set approximates the full Bayesian posterior predictive p(y|x, D) for that prior, with no retraining or MCMC per dataset. They show this recovers Gaussian process posteriors, Bayesian neural network posteriors, and few-shot classification, all as instances of the same recipe.

**Relevance / limitations:** This is the foundational mechanism underlying everything in this repo — the entire prior/PFN pairing in `src/ppfn/prior/bnn/` and `src/ppfn/model/pfn/`, and the bar-distribution PPD output used throughout `docs/ROADMAP.md` §3 and §5.3, is a direct instance of this paper's recipe. Its limitation for us is only that it is single-task/single-dataset by construction: the base PFN has no notion of two related-but-misaligned point clouds, no cross-cloud attention, and no registration machinery — which is exactly the gap `docs/ROADMAP.md` as a whole is trying to fill on top of it.

### PFNs4BO: In-Context Learning for Bayesian Optimization ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17535)) — [repo](https://github.com/automl/PFNs4BO)

**Idea:** Müller, Feurer, Hollmann & Hutter apply PFNs as BO surrogates: train on samples from priors that can reproduce GP-like and BNN-like behavior, and show the resulting PFN can be dropped directly into a standard BO loop's acquisition function in place of a GP posterior, additionally supporting extras a GP struggles with — injecting a user-specified prior over the optimum's location, and non-myopic (multi-step-lookahead) acquisition — via prior design rather than new inference machinery. Evaluated on HPO-B, Bayesmark, and PD1.

**Relevance / limitations:** Directly relevant as the template for "PFN as a drop-in BO surrogate" that this project's eventual registration-aware model would also need to be, once trained (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §7 inference, the warp-marginalized predictive distribution feeding an acquisition function). Its limitation for us is the same as the base PFN paper's: single-task, no notion of a second, differently-coordinated data source to transfer from — it is the surrogate half of the pipeline, not a transfer mechanism.

### ifBO: In-context Freeze-Thaw Bayesian Optimization for Hyperparameter Optimization ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16795), [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=VyoY3Wh9Wd), ICML 2024) — [repo](https://github.com/automl/ifBO)

**Idea:** A PFN (`FT-PFN`, the "freeze-thaw" surrogate) trained to predict a partially-observed learning curve's continuation given other curves' full or partial trajectories in-context, used inside a BO loop that can freeze an unpromising configuration's training and later thaw/resume it, or start a new one — amortizing the freeze-thaw GP machinery of classical multi-fidelity BO into a single forward pass. This is the codebase's namesake/ancestor: `PFNs4HPO`/`ifBO` is a direct dependency (`pyproject.toml`, `external/ifBO/`, `external/ifbo_icml2024/`), and `src/ppfn/prior/bnn/` and the trainer/callback scaffolding in `src/ppfn/trainer/` descend from this lineage (see `docs/labbook/2026-08-28-ifbo-mlp-lineage-and-seed-source-pattern.md`).

**Relevance / limitations:** It's the direct architectural and code ancestor of this repo rather than a method to reconstruct — the freeze-thaw *time* dimension (partial learning curves) is a different scarcity structure than the deformed-input-coordinates scarcity this project is now built around (`docs/ROADMAP.md` §1.1), but the "context = other trajectories" in-context-conditioning pattern is exactly what our A ∪ B context construction (§3.2a) generalizes. No coordinate warp, registration, or cross-domain transfer of any kind — every curve lives in the same hyperparameter space.

### TabPFN ([repo](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN); originally [automl/TabPFN](https://github.com/automl/TabPFN))

**Idea:** A PFN pretrained once on synthetic tabular-classification/regression tasks (drawn from a structural-causal-model prior), then applied in-context to a new small tabular dataset with no retraining. Not one fixed architecture — see version note below.

**Relevance / limitations — version lineage matters here, don't cite "TabPFN" as one method.** The repo pins `tabpfn>=8.1.0` (`pyproject.toml`) and there are checkpoints for two different lineages under `models/pfn_ckpt/` (`ftpfnv0.0.1.tar.gz` — ifBO's FT-PFN — and `bopfn_broken_unisep_1000curves_10params_2M.pt`), so it's worth being precise about which TabPFN generation any claim in this project refers to:

- **TabPFN (v1)** — Hollmann, Müller, Eggensperger & Hutter, ICLR 2023, ["TabPFN: A Transformer That Solves Small Tabular Classification Problems in a Second"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01848). Treats a row as a single set-valued token; no attention across the feature dimension.
- **TabPFN v2** — Hollmann, Müller et al., Nature 2025, ["Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model"](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6). **This is the version that introduces two-way attention** — alternating attention across samples (rows) and across features (columns) — plus a new prior and regression support. So the user's recollection ("feature attn starts at v2") is correct.
- **TabPFN-2.5** — ["Advancing the State of the Art in Tabular Foundation Models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08667), Nov 2025. Scales the same v2-style architecture to larger tables (up to 50k rows / 2k features).
- **TabPFN-3** — released May 2026 (Prior Labs technical report, no arXiv preprint found as of this writing), now the repo's default; up to 20x faster inference than 2.5 via a reduced KV cache and row-chunking, reportedly scaling to ~1M rows on a single H100.

For this project: the "multiple ones of them in the repo" the user noticed are these different checkpoint lineages, not copies of the same model — if anamorphic/TabPFN-block-design work (`archive/src/ppfn/model/anamorphic/`) resumes, it needs to state which TabPFN generation it's built against, since v1's lack of feature-attention vs. v2+'s dual attention is architecturally significant for any block-design/mixed-effects scheme that manipulates the feature axis.
